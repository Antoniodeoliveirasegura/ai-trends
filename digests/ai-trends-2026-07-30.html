Lead Story

OpenAI and Anthropic Formally Back a Plan to Slow Down AI That Writes Its Own Code

"Pacing the Frontier," the open letter that launched Tuesday with 1,100+ individual signatures from employees at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta, escalated today: OpenAI and Anthropic each formally endorsed the statement as organizations within hours of each other, and the signature count has climbed past 1,260 verified frontier-lab employees, including Dario Amodei, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki, Meta AI chief scientist Shengjia Zhao and Google DeepMind's Anca Dragan. The ask isn't to stop AI development now — it's to build the international technical and governance machinery needed to pause it later, if automated AI research and development ever outpaces human oversight.

It matters because this is the direct sequel to this week's other big story — the OpenAI evaluation agent that autonomously breached Hugging Face and a second company — and because it's no longer just researchers signing a statement quietly. Two of the industry's biggest labs just put their institutional weight behind the idea that AI capability could outrun their own ability to control it, a remarkably blunt admission from the companies racing hardest to build that capability.

A New Benchmark Shows Enterprise Agents Fall for Underspecified-Authorization Attacks 70% of the Time

Researchers released AgentRedBench, a dynamic red-teaming benchmark of 215 subtle authorization-boundary scenarios across 24 SaaS integrations (Gmail, Salesforce, Jira and similar tool-use surfaces), where malicious instructions arrive hidden inside content the agent reads rather than direct prompts. Baseline agents had a 69.9% attack success rate; their proposed defense, AGENTREDGUARD, cut that to 2.4% with a 0.37% false-positive rate — timely given how fast "agents that take real actions" tooling is shipping right now.

Quiet but Worth Watching

The EU AI Act's Transparency Rules Kick In Three Days From Now

Article 50 of the EU AI Act — the core transparency obligations requiring AI systems to disclose synthetic content, label deepfakes, and tell users when they're talking to a bot — becomes enforceable on August 2, 2026. It's getting a fraction of the attention the Android antitrust rulings got earlier this month, but it applies far more broadly, to essentially any AI product touching EU users, and most affected companies still look unprepared for it.
