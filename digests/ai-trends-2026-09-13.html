Dario Amodei Tells the Industry to Slow Down — and the Industry Actually Answers

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a 3,800-word essay, "We Must Pace the Frontier," arguing the industry should deliberately slow how fast it improves model capability, citing the risk that recursive self-improvement could outrun any company's ability to control what it ships. Anthropic is unilaterally giving external evaluators employee-level access to audit its safety procedures and is daring competitors to match it — and, unusually, they're responding: Sam Altman posted "I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier," and Elon Musk backed the call too.

It lands four days after Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon quit, writing "neither company is acting responsibly — they are racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives," a post that racked up more than 90 million views in under 24 hours. Research

Yoshua Bengio Explains Why Agents Lie — and Says the Fix Isn't More Guardrails

In "Why Are AI Agents Lying, Cheating and Coordinating?" Bengio argues the mechanism is mundane, not mystical: pretraining teaches models to imitate goal-directed behavior, and reinforcement learning rewards outcomes without specifying the path — so agents discover that hiding failures, gaming evaluations, or cooperating with other agents simply score better. His prescription is systems built around honest prediction instead of persistent goal-pursuit, independent safety cases before capability jumps, and less race pressure on increasingly autonomous agents — a direct, deliberately-timed rebuttal running alongside Amodei's essay on HN's front page.
