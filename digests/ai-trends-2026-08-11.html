Lead Story

An Unreleased Claude Model Nearly Doubles a 160-Year-Old Riemann Hypothesis Bound

Anthropic says an experimental, unreleased research version of Claude was set loose on the Riemann hypothesis and — while it didn't solve it — pushed the proven lower bound for the fraction of nontrivial zeta zeros lying on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2%, the largest single jump in the record's history. The run spanned 31 million output tokens across two sessions, roughly 60 subagents, 2,400 shell commands, and 54 arXiv papers reviewed, and Claude produced a machine-checkable Lean formalization of the argument. Anthropic mathematicians Levent Alpöge and Ralph Furman reviewed the work internally, and outside number theorists Brian Conrey and Dan Goldston examined it on short notice.

It matters because this is a genuine, expert-vetted research contribution to a famous open problem — not a benchmark score or a demo. It's not a proof (100% would be required, and conventional peer review hasn't happened yet), and the result can't be independently reproduced since the model itself isn't public. But a Menlo Ventures researcher called it "possibly the most significant breakthrough in analytic number theory since the 2013 bounded-prime-gaps result," and it lands the same week Anthropic is reshaping its cybersecurity, government, and infrastructure businesses — a striking coincidence of "Claude does real science" and "Claude is scaling fast" narratives on the same day.

Quiet but Worth Watching

A Munich Court Just Asserted EU Jurisdiction Over US AI Training

Buried under bigger headlines, a Munich court ruled AI music company Suno infringed copyright by training on works represented by German collecting society GEMA — and rejected the argument that training itself doesn't require a license. The bigger deal is jurisdictional: the court reasoned that serving outputs to EU users is enough to subject US-based training to EU law, regardless of where the model was actually built. If that reasoning holds on appeal, every US lab with EU users just inherited new legal exposure.
