Lead Story

Tesla's Fully Driverless Cybercab Hits Austin Streets — NHTSA Opens a Federal Safety Probe Within Hours

Tesla put roughly 1,000 Cybercabs — no steering wheel, no pedals — into commercial robotaxi service in Austin, and the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an investigation the same day into whether Tesla correctly self-certified that federal vehicle safety standards don't apply to a car with no manual controls at all. Unlike Zoox, which sought a formal federal exemption before deploying its own wheel-less robotaxi and still spent four years grounded during review, Tesla skipped that process entirely and went straight to public roads.

It matters because it's the first real regulatory test of whether AI-driven autonomy can leapfrog the certification path other companies treated as mandatory — the outcome will shape how every other AV operator times its own driverless rollout, and it lands in the same week OpenAI is asking the public to trust an AI system's own safety self-assessment (see Astra fallout, still rippling through Culture & Discourse below).

Agentic AI Tasks Can Carry Roughly 10,000x the Footprint of a Single Query
