Lead Story

SpaceX Closes Its $60B Acquisition of Cursor, Folding a Top AI Coding Tool Into the World's Largest GPU Fleet

SpaceX officially closed its all-stock acquisition of Cursor on August 15, converting Cursor's outstanding shares into roughly 389.3 million shares of SpaceX Class A common stock at an implied $60 billion equity value — a deal first struck as an option in April and made firm in June. Cursor's team is folding into SpaceXAI, where it will work on Grok Build, Grok Bot, and the Cursor editor itself, now backed by direct access to SpaceX's Colossus supercomputer, the compute cluster the rocket company already rents out to Anthropic and Google.

It matters because it's the starkest sign yet of AI's vertical consolidation: a rocket and satellite company now directly owns one of the most widely used AI-native code editors, collapsing the line between raw compute, frontier models, and the developer tools built on top of them. Elon Musk called it "an honour to have such a great team join SpaceX"; Cursor CEO Michael Truell said the company is "grateful" to become part of it.
