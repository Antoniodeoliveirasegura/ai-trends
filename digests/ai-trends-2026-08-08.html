AMD Buys a Startup That Etches AI Models Directly Into Chip Silicon

AMD is acquiring Canadian startup Taalas, whose chips hard-code a specific model's weights into the transistor connections themselves instead of storing them in memory — a demo chip hit 16,960 tokens/sec per user on Llama 3.1-8B, which AMD claims is 48x faster than Nvidia GPUs. The catch: each chip is locked to one model, so swapping models means swapping silicon — the second major "burn the model into hardware" bet this year, after Nvidia's Groq acquisition.

Appeals Court Sides With Perplexity's AI Shopping Agent Over Amazon

The Ninth Circuit vacated a preliminary injunction that had blocked Perplexity's Comet browser from shopping Amazon on users' behalf, finding it's the user — not Perplexity — who "accesses" Amazon's systems under the CFAA. It's the first federal appellate ruling on whether AI agents can legally act on a user's behalf on third-party platforms, a foundational precedent for the emerging "agentic commerce" fight.

Quiet but Worth Watching

Nvidia Open-Sources NOOA — an AI Agent Defined as a Single Python Class

Rolled out under Nvidia's 37-member Open Secure AI Alliance, NOOA (Nvidia Object-Oriented Agents) collapses an entire agent's capabilities, state, and prompts into one Apache-2.0 Python class — methods are capabilities, docstrings are prompts, type annotations are enforced contracts. Nvidia claims a 253-line reference agent hits 82.2% on SWE-bench Verified with GPT-5.5 while cutting token usage/cost by up to 50%. It hasn't gone viral yet, but it's a notably opinionated, auditable take on agent-harness design from the industry's biggest hardware player.
