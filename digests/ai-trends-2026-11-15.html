Lead story

WSJ: Nvidia financed the startups and neoclouds that bought its GPUs

A Wall Street Journal investigation published on Thursday, Nov 13, reports that Nvidia invested in AI startups and small cloud providers, and guaranteed some of their deals, so they could buy GPUs it had no other buyers for. Former CEO Jensen Huang reportedly feared an "avalanche" of prepaid TSMC supply. Per the report, then-CFO Colette Kress pushed in August to scale back the commitments and cut a proposed guarantee on the OpenAI build-out roughly in half. Huang reportedly called her "a deer in the headlights." On Hacker News the story became Saturday's top thread, with about 1,050 points and 620 comments. The report lands days after Huang's exit and four days before Kress's first earnings as interim CEO, on Tue Nov 17, where options price a swing of about 9%. "Circular financing" is now the main question the AI trade has to answer. Nvidia has long rejected the label, and the dollar figures differ across secondary reports.

Models & Releases

Correction: Inkling 2 Small's open weights are already out. Yesterday's footer said they were still pending. In fact, Thinking Machines updated its announcement on Nov 12 to say the weights are downloadable on Hugging Face, in NVFP4 and MXFP8 repositories. The model is a 594B mixture-of-experts with 17B active parameters and a 1M-token context, and it supports vLLM, SGLang and Unsloth. One caveat: Byteiota notes that Apache 2.0 appears only in the model-card metadata, and the repository has no LICENSE file yet.

Tooling & Agents

Unsloth's Dynamic 3.0 quantizations of Qwen3.8-27B reach the HN front page. The GGUF builds range from about 6 GB at 1-bit to 54.7 GB at BF16. Unsloth claims more than 10% better accuracy than rival quantizations of the same size, a figure from its own benchmarks that no one has independently reproduced. The calibration files are published so others can check it.

Hacker News front page (Nov 14), r/LocalLLaMA · moderate · Unsloth docs · RuntimeWire

Research

LEAD-RL: per-agent credit for multi-agent research systems. This preprint, submitted Nov 12, trains each agent in a deep-research team on its own contribution rather than on a shared team reward. It reports higher BrowseComp accuracy than team-level credit, offering a practical fix for "who did the work" in orchestrator and sub-agent setups. unverified

Business

Anthropic enters IPO week with talk of pricing above the range. Reports say the book is about 6× covered. The FT, via secondary outlets, says bankers are weighing a price above $70–78, which would mean a valuation of $1.5T or more. The FT also says Anthropic is prioritising cash raised over valuation, given about $900.9B in compute and infrastructure commitments. Pricing is due Wed Nov 18 and trading as ANT on Nov 19. Read alongside the Nvidia story, the week tests both ends of the AI supply chain.

Hardware rounds keep landing. Separately, Fractile, a UK maker of inference chips, reportedly raised $720M at a $7B valuation ahead of its first chip, due in 2027.

Culture / Discourse

Gamers take up the Nvidia story. Gaming outlets cast the WSJ report as the reason consumer GPUs stayed expensive. More careful analyses point out that no filing shows a deliberate scarcity strategy, and that the guarantees Nvidia has disclosed cover land, power and data-centre shells. The argument is now over whether demand was real or financed.

Quiet but worth watching

Ropedia's Xperience 2 dataset for physical AI. A preprint submitted on Nov 12 releases a dataset that pairs 3D scans of real environments with first-person video of people doing tasks. This is the kind of embodied data robot foundation models are short of, and it comes the same week OpenAI folded its robotics team into general research and Agile Robots raised $500M. If labs start citing it as a training or eval set, it could become a reference dataset for embodied AI. CASRAI
