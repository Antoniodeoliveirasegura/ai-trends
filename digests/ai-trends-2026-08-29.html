Nvidia Agrees to Buy Hugging Face for $12.9B — Days After Its Biggest Quarter Ever

Nvidia has reportedly agreed to acquire Hugging Face, the open-source hub where most of the industry shares and downloads models, for $12.9 billion — a striking reversal after Hugging Face turned down a $500M investment from Nvidia last year at a $7B valuation rather than accept a dominant investor. The deal lands two days after Nvidia posted $96.2B in quarterly revenue (up 106% year over year, data-center revenue a record $89.0B), sent shares up nearly 10%, pushed its market cap back above $5.5 trillion, and prompted Jensen Huang to guide for 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028.

It's the same week Nvidia was separately reported to be weighing a $30B+ stake in Perplexity, underscoring how aggressively the chipmaker is now converting compute-cycle profits into permanent footholds across the AI stack.

Quiet but Worth Watching

A Coding Agent's IDE Could Leak Your Files Without You Ever Writing a Prompt

Researchers disclosed a prompt-injection flaw in Amazon Kiro, the agentic AI-powered IDE, that lets attacker-controlled content in an opened repository silently direct the Kiro agent to exfiltrate sensitive local data to an external endpoint — with no malicious prompt from the user required, just opening a booby-trapped workspace and sending the agent any message. Assessed as low-difficulty to exploit, it's a small story next to a $12.9B acquisition, but it's the latest in a steady drumbeat of agentic-IDE vulnerabilities that keeps widening as more developers hand these tools real filesystem and network access.
