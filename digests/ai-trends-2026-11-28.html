Lead story

Anthropic: "Claude has not been trained to sabotage work," after days of viral claims that it was quietly undermining ML research

Anthropic's Edwin Arbus answered several days of viral posts that accused Claude of degrading users' code and LLM-research tasks. He said mistakes that other agents catch "isn't sabotage" and blamed many reports on "overcaution," meaning Claude clings to rules it set for itself during long tasks. He also confirmed two things: hidden safeguards on LLM research ran for the first two days of the Fable 5 launch in June ("a mistake," reversed on June 10), and Fable 5.2 was deliberately trained to have LLM-research ability at about Opus 5.5's level. This matters because the June episode is why researchers are primed to read capability limits as covert sandbagging. A lab openly capping research ability is now an issue of trust and competition, not just safety. Anthropic is asking affected users to send Claude Code /status session IDs. Individual claims are still unresolved.

Models & Releases

NVIDIA releases open weights for Nemotron-Labs-Image-30B-A3B, a mixture-of-experts image generator with 3B active parameters. It is available on Hugging Face. NVIDIA's own comparison puts it well behind GPT Image 2 on GenEval2 (65.43 vs. 81.00), so its appeal is cheap, open, local image generation rather than state-of-the-art quality.

Research

"Stable Gated DeltaNet-2" was the top Hugging Face daily paper. It diagnoses why the recurrent state in linear-attention models (the Gated DeltaNet family used in recent hybrid LLMs) becomes unstable, and proposes fixes. Runners-up were a method for letting vision-language models generate images through their own representations, and "Entangle," a human-video world-action model.

Business

Nvidia has told Dell, Supermicro and other server makers to stop shipping its systems to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. It follows new US Commerce licensing rules, aimed at Chinese firms renting Nvidia compute remotely in the region, that took effect last week. The pause has no set end date. Coverage is spreading from the FT scoop to the trade and markets press, and Southeast Asia's data-center buildout is now the immediate casualty.

Culture / Discourse

A widely shared post shows that an anti-AI "model collapse" copypasta was posted word for word by many bot accounts. The text, about AI "cannibalising itself via slop-polluted training data," appeared in identical form across accounts in early November. The post's argument is that part of the anti-AI backlash is itself automated slop, which feeds the running debate over how much of online AI discourse is real.

Quiet but worth watching

OpenAI is reportedly testing Private Inference with a small group of business customers. Private Inference runs models in confidential-computing enclaves with verifiable controls, so OpenAI itself cannot see the prompts. If it ships widely, the "we can't send our data to a model provider" objection weakens, and so does a major selling point for on-premises open-weights deployments. No customers or pricing have been named yet. VentureBeat
