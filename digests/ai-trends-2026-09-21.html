Lead Story

OpenAI's Hidden "__obi" Cookie Is Tracking ChatGPT Users Across the Web

Independent researcher Buchodi published an analysis showing OpenAI's ad-measurement infrastructure sets a year-long cookie, __obi, that ties activity on advertiser sites back to a person's ChatGPT account — reproduced independently across roughly 936 advertiser pixels on over 1,000 hostnames, and working even for logged-out users via a persistent device identifier that survives 27+ days. The thread hit the top of Hacker News with over 570 points and 300+ comments, the platform's loudest debate of the day, because OpenAI's own policy classifies the cookie as "analytics" rather than "marketing" — meaning it appears to have synced even when users explicitly denied marketing consent. It's the sharpest test yet of whether "the AI company that isn't an ad company" can hold that line once ChatGPT ads are actually running at scale.

Research

Google's Dream-RSI Lets Agents "Dream" Through Past Runs Instead of Re-Executing Them

Dream-RSI turns a discovery agent's history of past attempts into a replay simulator: new exploration strategies are scored against that recorded history rather than run for real, and only the best-scoring strategy actually executes. Because the model weights never change — just the exploration-policy code — the approach needed up to 162x fewer discovery-agent calls than the prior baseline (SimpleTES) while matching or beating its results, cutting iteration counts by as much as 2.43x.
