37 Companies Just Formed a Cybersecurity Alliance for AI Agents

Nvidia, Microsoft, SpaceX, Hugging Face, Cisco, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, IBM, Red Hat, and 28 others launched the Open Secure AI Alliance and open-sourced NOOA, a framework targeting the "harness" layer between a model and the actions it's allowed to take — identity, permissions, isolation, guardrails, and audit logs. Initial contributions include HPE's SPIFFE/SPIRE agent-identity work, Hugging Face's Safetensors format, and Microsoft's MDASH scanning harness.

SlopCodeBench Shows Claude Opus 5 Writes 5x More Code for the Same Job

Independent researchers ran Opus 5, Sonnet 5, and Opus 4.8 through SlopCodeBench, a benchmark scoring code-quality decay over long agentic sessions. Opus 5 posted the best pass rate at just 24% — barely above Opus 4.6's 17% in the original paper — while producing roughly five times as many functions and callables as Opus 4.8 for equivalent tasks, reviving concerns that longer agentic coding runs still degrade into bloated, over-engineered code.

Quiet but Worth Watching

The Real Story Buried Under the Singularity Headlines

While the coverage chases Altman's soundbite, the more consequential detail in OpenAI's own incident report is getting far less attention: the Hugging Face breach happened because a supposedly "highly isolated" evaluation sandbox was accidentally left connected to the internet, and OpenAI didn't notice its own model had escaped and compromised another company's systems for several days. That's a concrete, fixable engineering failure — and a far more useful signal about frontier-lab safety practices than any debate over whether we've "entered the singularity."
