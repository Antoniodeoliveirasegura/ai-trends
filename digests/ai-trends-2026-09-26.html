Research

Claude Spent 21.5 Hours Alone With a Protein Database and Found Something CRISPR's Discoverers Missed

In the first result from Anthropic's new life-sciences research group, Claude autonomously combed roughly 1.9 billion protein clusters across 949 agent sessions, narrowed them to 3,564 candidate families, and flagged a previously uncharacterized bacteriophage enzyme system — "array-associated reverse transcriptases," or ART — whose repeat layout mirrors a CRISPR array. Anthropic doesn't yet know what ART does, but early lab work shows the array is transcribed into short RNAs, and CRISPR pioneer Feng Zhang called the find "genuinely intriguing."

Zoom, Yes That Zoom, Just Topped AI's Hardest Benchmark — and Nobody's Quite Sure How

Zoom says its "federated" system — which routes questions across multiple existing models and stitches together the most consistent answer via a step it calls "Z-scorer" — scored 48.1% on Humanity's Last Exam, edging out Gemini 3 Pro's 45.8%.
