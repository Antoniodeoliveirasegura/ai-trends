Lead story

OpenAI launches ChatGPT Wallet, letting its "dots" agents pay for things

On Friday, Nov 13, OpenAI began a gradual rollout of ChatGPT Wallet to US ChatGPT Pro users aged 18 and over. It works with eligible US-issued American Express, Chase, Mastercard and Visa cards. For now, every purchase needs approval: the user reviews the items, total and card, then confirms with Face ID or a fingerprint. The card network then issues a single-use token, so neither the merchant nor the agent sees the real card number. Reports say OpenAI expects to allow pre-approved spending within limits such as caps later. It is the first mass-market product to give AI agents payment rails, and it arrives one day after United Airlines said it would cancel bookings made by agents. Expect pressure on merchants to pick a side on agent shopping before the holidays.

Research

LLMs design better quantum circuits. In a QuEra, Harvard and Anthropic preprint (arXiv:2610.22367), circuits designed by AI beat the standard designs on both gate count and qubit count for seven of eight fault-tolerant building blocks. Few results show AI improving on expert designs in a hard engineering field this concretely.

OpenAI folds its stand-alone robotics team into its main organisation, Fortune reported as an exclusive. Robotics researchers now report to Chief Research Officer Mark Chen, and the applied robotics and hardware staff move to Greg Brockman's Applied organisation. OpenAI says robotics "remains a core part" of its plans and argues that general-purpose models now run its robots. It is the second time OpenAI has wound down a dedicated robotics team.

Quiet but worth watching

The weights for Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Small are due "mid-November." The model is a 594B mixture-of-experts with 17B active parameters. It launched on Nov 5 with closed weights, available only through the Tinker platform, and the lab has promised an Apache 2.0 release once outside red-teaming is finished. If the release happens on schedule, it would be one of the largest permissively licensed models from a US lab. That would matter for the debate over US vs. Chinese open-weight models.
