A Lawsuit Over AI-Picked Layoffs Is Quietly Testing the First State Laws Built for This

Twenty-six Meta employees are suing in federal court in Oakland, alleging the company's layoff algorithm — fed by AI-token-usage dashboards, keystroke monitoring, and performance scores — disproportionately selected workers on medical, family, or disability leave, in violation of new California and New York City laws specifically written to govern automated employment decisions. It's barely registering next to this week's IPO and funding headlines, but as more companies quietly hand layoff decisions to models, this case is shaping up to be one of the first real tests of whether "the algorithm did it" holds up in court.
