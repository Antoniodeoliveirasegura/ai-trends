Lead Story

Security Researchers Used Claude to Break Into OpenAI's Internal Systems

A three-person team at security startup Hacktron AI, working inside OpenAI's own bug-bounty program, chained an SSO misconfiguration with an RCE flaw in Discourse (the forum software behind OpenAI's community site) to reach employee accounts and OpenAI's internal code repository — start to finish in under 72 hours. Claude Opus 4.8 could find the flaw but not reliably exploit it; after Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5, the same researchers had a working exploit in about three hours. OpenAI patched the hole within 14 hours and paid a $6,500 bounty, and no code was actually exfiltrated — but it's a vivid, fully-documented case of one lab's frontier model being turned into an offensive tool against a rival, landing in the same week OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind are trying to stand up a joint safety self-governance body.

Research

A Fingerprint for Every Model — Even the Ones Built by Distilling Yours

AttnPrint extracts the low-frequency components of a model's cross-modal attention as a white-box fingerprint; the companion black-box method, DistillTrace, uses output hypothesis-testing to catch distillation relationships without any internal access. Tested across 154 model instances spanning 19 architectures, both held up against five separate attempts to modify or disguise the derivative model — timely groundwork for the growing pile of "you distilled our model" disputes.

Quiet but Worth Watching

"Harm Laundering": Safety Training May Be Hiding Bias, Not Removing It

Researchers Sarah Wyer, Sue Black and Noura Al Moubayed argue that the surface-form classifiers labs use to show declining harm scores across model generations are measuring the wrong thing: explicit gender-discriminatory content that was obvious in GPT-2/GPT-3-era outputs simply becomes less detectable by GPT-4 and GPT-5, not less present. It only pulled a modest Hacker News score (~25) so far, but if the methodology holds up it undercuts a core piece of evidence every lab points to when claiming safety progress.
