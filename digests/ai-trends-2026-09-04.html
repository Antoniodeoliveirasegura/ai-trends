Lead Story

OpenAI Ships GPT-6 Astra — Its First "Critical"-Rated Model — and Greg Brockman Calls It "the Start of the AGI Era"

OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 Astra on Thursday, first to vetted defenders in its Daybreak cybersecurity program before wider paid access, after formally classifying it "Critical" under its Preparedness Framework — the first model the company says can autonomously discover unknown flaws and build working exploits against hardened, well-defended systems without step-by-step human direction. OpenAI president Greg Brockman went further, saying that "if we fast forward a couple years... I think it might be about this model" when asked whether Astra represents AGI, citing its superhuman speed at spreadsheets, forms, and browser navigation, plus claimed proofs of decades-old math problems.

It matters because it turns two live debates from abstract to concrete in a single day: whether frontier AI has crossed into genuinely dangerous cyber-offense territory, and whether "AGI" is now a marketing label rather than a measurable milestone. Both fights immediately got messier — mathematicians say some of Astra's ten "new" proofs lean on uncredited prior work, and safety researchers say the model's reasoning technique makes it harder to monitor (see Culture & Discourse, below) — so today's launch reads less like a victory lap and more like the opening move in a much longer argument.

"Totally Destroy CoT Monitorability" — Safety Researchers Sharpen Their Warning on Astra's Reasoning Style

Astra's "recurrent depth" technique does more reasoning in latent space, and Redwood Research CEO Buck Shlegeris warned that if OpenAI scales the approach further it could "massively increase the recurrence and totally destroy" the ability to monitor a model's chain of thought. OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki says the company has "worked to preserve and utilize" CoT monitoring since its first reasoning models, but former OpenAI researcher Daniel Kokotajlo's rebuttal — that even if OpenAI holds the line, "others might" — is the line spreading fastest today.

Quiet but Worth Watching

A Third of Companies Are Quietly Skipping the Software Purchase Entirely

McKinsey's 2026 State of AI survey (1,719 respondents, 97 countries) found 32% of organizations have skipped buying at least one software product because agentic coding tools let them build it in-house instead — rising to nearly half among the small group of "high performers" who say AI already moves their bottom line, and topping out at 41% in tech specifically.
