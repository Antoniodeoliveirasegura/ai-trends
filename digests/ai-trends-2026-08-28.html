An Indian Power Company Just Placed an $8B Order for Nvidia Chips That Haven't Even Shipped Yet

AM Intelligence, the AI-infrastructure arm of renewable-energy giant Greenko Group, has placed a binding order for roughly 9,000 Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 rack-scale systems — Nvidia's next-generation platform, successor to Blackwell — as part of an $8 billion plan to bring 1 gigawatt of AI computing capacity online at a Hyderabad site by 2030. Delivery is slated for Q1 2027, with the first 200MW tranche already sold to an undisclosed US customer under NDA, and the facility is designed to run trillion-parameter models and agentic AI workloads.

It matters because it's one of the largest single commercial commitments yet to hardware that isn't in mass production — a bet that demand for frontier-scale compute keeps outrunning supply well into 2027, and a sign that the AI buildout race is no longer confined to US hyperscalers and Gulf sovereign funds. It lands days after Nvidia warned contract manufacturers that Vera Rubin server prices are rising 15%+ on memory costs alone, making this order a costly vote of confidence in exactly the chip getting more expensive.

A Third of American Adults Now Ask a Chatbot About Their Symptoms

A Pew Research survey of 3,488 US adults finds 34% now use AI chatbots for at least one health-related task — mostly quick health information (28%), figuring out what's causing symptoms (25%), and low-cost information access (22%) — with adoption highest among Asian Americans (56%) and adults under 30 (44%). Only 47% call the answers very helpful and just 29% feel comfortable sharing personal health data with the tools, and a companion Pew report finds Americans are more likely to say chatbots hurt than help people using them for loneliness, depression, or stress.

Quiet but Worth Watching

Invisible Text in an Email Just Showed How Easily an AI Assistant Can Be Tricked

Forcepoint X-Labs demonstrated that instructions hidden in an email via invisible HTML text — unreadable to a human, fully readable to an AI summarizer — can silently hijack an assistant's output. In their test case, the planted text got an AI summarizer to move an invoice deadline from August 21 to September 3 without the recipient noticing anything unusual in the visible message. It's a small, unglamorous proof-of-concept next to a chip order or a model launch, but as AI email and document summarizers get embedded deeper into enterprise workflows, this is the kind of quiet prompt-injection research that tends to precede a much louder incident.
