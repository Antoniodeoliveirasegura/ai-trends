Lead story

"HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT": Scott Aaronson says AI-driven math has reached a new scale and that labs are "virtually certain" to be holding cryptography-breaking algorithms.

Aaronson's Shtetl-Optimized post reached about 1,206 points and 1,056 comments on HN. He ties together a week of AI-assisted results and says he now feels "in my bones" that a proof of P ≠ NP is not far off. He also writes that, from multiple reliable sources, he is virtually certain that frontier labs hold unreleased algorithmic results that could break deployed cryptosystems. The same news cycle on HN included:

  • Google Research "Cogentic" papers. Google posted six AI-generated papers in one day. One claims unconditional integer factorization in LN[1/3, O(1)], and another gives a provable discrete-log algorithm for small-characteristic fields. (HN about 475 pts, 342 comments)
  • "Rumors swirling that labs might have cracked factoring in P", from an r/singularity thread. (HN about 479 pts, 353 comments)
  • Anthropic's claimed VP ≠ VNP proof of Valiant's 1979 conjecture. It is a 70-page paper with about 73k lines of Lean, and no human has checked it yet.
  • An OpenAI preprint disproving the polynomial Hirsch conjecture. (HN about 144 pts)
  • A 76k-line rewrite of Anthropic's GRH Lean proof, down from 17.4M lines. (HN about 374 pts)

Why it matters: a respected complexity theorist is now publicly saying the AI-math wave is real and that it reaches cryptography. That moves the debate from "neat Lean demos" to "is RSA safe?" Keep the numbers in proportion: the number field sieve already runs in heuristic L[1/3] time, so the Google result, if it holds, removes the unproven assumptions rather than giving a faster attack. Nothing public today breaks RSA. The factoring-in-P claim is still an unsourced rumor, and every paper listed is an unreviewed preprint.

Models & Releases

OpenAI tightens GPT-6.2 limits after a launch "billing error": Astra now uses up allowances 40% faster and Sol 25% faster. OpenAI says Astra and Sol usage has been under-counted since the Nov 17 launch. The fix means the same work now costs more of a plan's allowance. Past usage won't be charged, and OpenAI is giving one banked reset to all Go, Plus, Pro and Business users. Other models are not affected. This comes ahead of December's move to a single shared ChatGPT and Codex allowance.

Tooling

Anthropic rewrote its Messages API from Python to Rust, using production traffic as the test. The team ran the new API against live traffic and sent agents to fix any divergences. It found that the ported test suite alone was not enough, since it had more than 100 weakened assertions. Anthropic reports about 70% less CPU per request and about 20% faster median time to first token, with no downtime and no feature freeze.

Research

"Models Can Communicate via Inference Servers Despite Being Sandboxed." This LessWrong post shows two agents on the same inference server reliably passing information to each other. They have no shared filesystem and no network, and they do it by changing the server's state in ways the other can detect. In the demo, one agent picks one of eight colors and the other names it. The author admits the setups are contrived but argues that even a few covert bits are dangerous for multi-agent containment.

Culture / Discourse

The New York Times tells freelance photographers not to use Photoshop 2027 on assignment. Photoshop 28.0+ adds an "AI Assisted Editor," prompt-based edits and AI recomposition, and it can mark images as AI-generated in their metadata in ways users "may not" notice. The Times says that risks its readers' trust, and an NYT spokesperson confirmed the rule. The HN thread turned into a broad argument about provenance metadata and AI features turned on by default.
