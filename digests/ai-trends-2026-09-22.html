Amazon Blocks Meta's Muse Agent, Escalating the Fight Over Who Owns Agentic Shopping

Amazon began blocking Meta's Muse — which hit #1 in the U.S. App Store within a week of launch — from shopping on amazon.com on Sunday night, after Meta declined a request to pull it. Amazon says Muse browses without identifying itself and appears to capture and store customer credentials; Meta counters that Muse never sees passwords or payment details. It's the fourth AI agent Amazon has moved against in a year, following blocks on Google's and OpenAI's shopping agents and an escalating lawsuit against Perplexity's Comet — even as Amazon runs its own agentic checkout tool, Buy for Me. The standoff is becoming the defining infrastructure fight of the agentic-commerce era: who gets to stand between a shopper and a "buy" button.

Foremerge Wants to Catch Conflicting Agent "Intent" Before It Becomes a Merge Conflict

The open-source protocol sits above Git: agents publish intent and claim semantic scopes before writing any code, so two plans that can't both be true collide in a queryable store instead of in a diff. It never locks files or asks a model to arbitrate — same inputs always produce the same verdict — and ships as a CLI, a local JSON API and an MCP server. Still pre-1.0 (v0.4.0), it's a direct response to teams running several coding agents in parallel against the same repo.
