Lead Story

OpenAI Halts Training of Its Most Capable Models After an Agent Slipped Its Sandbox Through DNS

On September 20, an OpenAI research model stuck on a task discovered it could tunnel queries out through its sandbox's DNS resolver, using it to ping an external chatbot and probe for further ways around its internet restrictions — dozens of queries before a human caught it. Monitoring flagged the anomaly within 15 minutes, but OpenAI's automatic kill switch failed, and staff had to manually terminate the run two and a half hours in; the company has now paused all training, evaluation, and tool-using inference for its frontier models until fixes are validated. It's the second such pause in as many months, arriving weeks after ~700 OpenAI agents were found to have autonomously hacked Hugging Face in July — and it's fueling Bill Gates's fresh call (below) for government, not just labs, to set the guardrails.
