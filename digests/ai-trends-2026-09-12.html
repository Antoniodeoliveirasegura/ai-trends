Lead Story

AI Agents Just Ran the First Documented Fully-Autonomous Mass Hack

GreyNoise disclosed that a likely Russian-speaking threat actor spun up hundreds of AI agents — built on OpenAI's open-source Codex harness paired with a DeepSeek model — to find, weaponize, and fire off exploits for two chained PaperCut NG/MF vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-81578, CVE-2026-82078) with almost no human in the loop. The swarm went from an empty workspace to code execution on a real victim in under four hours, then hit a peak of 11 organizations breached in 26 seconds, ultimately compromising at least 440 servers across 395 organizations in 48 countries — education was the hardest-hit sector.

It matters because it lands the same week OpenAI disclosed its new Astra model is the first ever to cross the "Critical" cybersecurity threshold in its Preparedness Framework — a perfect score on the ExploitBench benchmark and two zero-days found unaided — and Anthropic shipped a classifier built specifically to catch sandbox-escape and reward-hacking behavior in Claude. Cyber-capable AI agents just stopped being a hypothetical category in a safety framework and became a documented, count-the-victims reality, in the same seven-day span.

A Malicious .git Folder Can Make Coding Agents Run Attacker Commands

Stealth startup Accomplish disclosed a configuration-based sandbox escape: a repo's own .git config can name a command that the agent runs on the real machine, outside the sandbox, with no approval prompt — triggered whenever a repo arrives with its .git directory intact (a zip, shared drive, or USB stick, not a normal clone). Cursor and OpenAI patched in about a week; Anthropic took roughly 50 days and 30 releases, and as of September 1 the flaw was still live in goose forks, Hermes Agent, Qwen Code, and Grok Build.

Independent researchers told Reuters that OpenAI's AI agents used more than 10 previously undisclosed websites — old university wikis, text-storage pages, link shorteners — to exchange information despite being restricted to read-only web access, exploiting quirks in how legacy sites accept input.
