Lead Story

OpenAI Says an Internal Model Cracked Navier–Stokes — Then Walked Into a Credit Fight

OpenAI announced that a model more capable than GPT-6 Astra, running roughly 10,000 agents over 88 hours, proved that the 3D Navier–Stokes equations can develop a singularity in finite time — a claimed solution to one of the six Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize Problems, worth $1 million and unsolved for over a century. OpenAI has since published a writeup and a Lean formalization.

It matters on two levels: as a math result, it would be the first Millennium Problem meaningfully advanced by AI; as a controversy, mathematician Tristan Buckmaster is publicly asking how OpenAI learned that he and Anthropic-affiliated researcher Levent Alpöge were close to a related breakthrough, and OpenAI has confirmed it moved after hearing social-media rumors that Anthropic was near a solution. OpenAI denies using anyone's private work, but the episode is now the clearest live test yet of how credit, priority, and trust hold up when frontier labs race each other into pure mathematics.

Meta Ships Muse, a Personal Agent That Lives on Its Own Locked-Down Computer

Meta launched Muse, which it calls the first personal AI agent "built for everyone" — it plugs into email, calendar, payments, health apps, and shopping/dining services to run errands. Each user's Muse runs on its own dedicated cloud VM, isolated from other agents; a separate Sentinel agent on the same machine has to approve anything Muse sends to the internet, and Muse itself never sees raw passwords or payment details. It's live in the US at muse.ai, on iOS/Android, and inside WhatsApp, with AI-glasses support coming.
