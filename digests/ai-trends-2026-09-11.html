Lead Story

OpenAI Reverses Course, Asks Congress for Mandatory National AI Safety Rules

OpenAI's chief global affairs officer Chris Lehane called for binding, capability-based federal AI safety regulation — testing protocols, independent model assessments, tougher cybersecurity requirements, and mandatory incident reporting — urging lawmakers to act before Congress adjourns in December. It's a stark reversal for a company that has spent the past two years resisting exactly this kind of rulemaking, and openly warning that early regulation would hand the lead to China.

It matters because the timing is not a coincidence: the U-turn lands two days after Anthropic's own alignment lead said he sees a greater-than-10% chance AI kills humanity within a decade, and a departing Anthropic researcher accused labs of "gambling with our lives." Critics on X immediately called the move hypocritical — pointing to OpenAI's own safety team reportedly "struggling for compute" in recent months — but the reversal, alongside California's new AI-auditor law signed two days earlier, signals the regulatory floor under frontier AI is shifting fast, from Sacramento up to Washington.
