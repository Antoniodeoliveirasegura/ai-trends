Lead story

Nvidia slides into its first earnings without Huang, the day before Anthropic prices its IPO

Nvidia shares fell about 13% last week, the biggest weekly drop since April 2025, after Jensen Huang resigned. They were down another ~2–3% in premarket trading Monday. Interim CEO Colette Kress reports fiscal Q3 results Tuesday after the close, and one preview expects about $109B in revenue. The next day, Wed Nov 18, Anthropic prices 825M shares at $70–78, roughly a $1.4T valuation, down from earlier talk of about $2T. Trading is due to start Thursday. The two events are linked: Nvidia is due to buy about $10B of Anthropic stock in the placement that runs alongside the IPO. Any guidance wobble on Tuesday therefore hits both the chip supplier and its biggest AI customer bet, the week after the WSJ's "circular financing" report.

Tooling & Agents

Cursor: a stolen Fireworks AI key led to a backdoored "Claude Code" extension. Cursor's Nov 13 incident update says an access key it had issued to inference provider Fireworks AI was exposed in a Fireworks breach. An attacker used it to reach Cursor data, then on Oct 29 planted a tampered Claude Code extension in the cache behind Cursor's marketplace. The extension tried to install persistent SSH backdoors, and up to ~5,000 developers may have installed it. Cursor says Anthropic's own extension, Privacy Mode users, customer code and official app updates were not affected, and warns that uninstalling alone may not remove the backdoor. Security outlets kept picking up the story over the weekend. It is the clearest case yet of an AI vendor's supply chain reaching developer machines.

Culture / Discourse

The FAA's AI tool takes blame for a nationwide ground stop. On Nov 13 the US had its first full ground stop since January 2023. It came three days after the FAA expanded SMART, an AI air-traffic planning tool built by Air Space Intelligence under an $875M contract, to all 22 en-route centers. The FAA cites a telecom outage, and Secretary Duffy says SMART played no role. The vendor denies causing it. Lawmakers who had already asked for a pause in October say they "can't rule out anything." The cause is still unconfirmed, but SMART has become a test case for AI in safety-critical public infrastructure.

Claude for Manufacturing and a "Model Hardware Standard." Anthropic is holding a webinar on Tue Nov 17 about a research preview in which plant equipment tells Claude what it can and cannot do, under what it calls a Model Hardware Standard, with an application process for participants.
