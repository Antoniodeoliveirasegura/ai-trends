Lead Story

SB Energy Files to Go Public, Revealing OpenAI Holds $5.5B in Warrants From Its Own Landlord

SoftBank-controlled SB Energy filed for a Nasdaq IPO targeting $5B–$7B, and the prospectus disclosed that OpenAI — the data-center operator's biggest prospective tenant — has been issued roughly $5.5 billion in stock warrants, on top of a $500 million direct investment made earlier this year alongside SoftBank as part of the Stargate buildout. Nvidia is separately committing up to $3 billion, split between a private placement and a prepaid forward contract, while SB Energy states outright that it is "substantially dependent" on OpenAI as both tenant and equity holder — and that none of its data-center capacity is operational yet, with first revenue not expected until Q4.

It matters because the filing is the clearest paper trail yet for the "circular AI deals" critique that's been building all year: the chipmaker, the landlord, and the model company are now financially entangled in the same transaction, each helping inflate the others' valuations ahead of a public offering. Supporters call it a "virtuous circle" that lines up suppliers and demand; critics see a mirage of growth that could unwind fast if AI spending ever cools — and this is the first time the mechanics have shown up in black-and-white SEC language rather than analyst speculation.
