Lead story

OpenAI publishes its pretraining loss curve and proposes perplexity as a shared limit on frontier AI development

In "Perplexity and Intelligence" (Nov 22), chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published OpenAI's internal held-out pretraining perplexity from GPT-3.5 onward. He argues that the metric tracks capability: according to coverage, losses below about 0.8 coincided with new abilities in cybersecurity and mathematics, and OpenAI expects further "transformative" capabilities at roughly 0.68–0.7 log-perplexity. He proposes that labs commit to common limits, based on perplexity and other measurements, that they would not cross until monitoring of AI behavior reaches certain milestones. Why it matters: a frontier lab is offering a single number that could be audited and written into treaties or regulation, rather than benchmark scores that are easy to game. Caveats: critics point out that pretraining loss misses gains from reasoning, RL, tools and scaffolding, which is where much of the recent progress has come from. The figures are also OpenAI's own and come from an evaluation set nobody else can see.

Research

"Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights." In arXiv 2611.21885, an open 27B model is given its own weights, its graded answers and the ability to run itself. It edits its weights directly, with no gradient training, and reportedly improves by 6.6 points on held-out problems. The authors say the effect grows with model scale. The HN thread is split between "a path to continual learning" and "an alignment problem in preprint form." These are the authors' own claims and have not been reproduced.
