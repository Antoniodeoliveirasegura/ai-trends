Nvidia Recruits Wall Street for $500 Billion AI Financing Push — Then Watches Its Stock Fall on "Circular Deal" Fears

Nvidia signed memorandums of understanding with six financial heavyweights — Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock's Global Infrastructure Partners, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR — to source $500 billion in financing that lets frontier labs, enterprises, and cloud providers buy Nvidia chips on credit. CEO Jensen Huang framed it as turning GPUs into a new asset class, financeable "like toll roads or power plants," and said he approached only these six firms and none turned him down.

It matters because the market's reaction undercuts the pitch: Nvidia shares fell 2.86% the same day, and analysts immediately flagged the structure as a variant of the "circular financing" the IMF and Bank for International Settlements have already called a systemic risk — a chipmaker helping fund the very customers who buy its chips, making real demand harder to measure. Huang spent the day insisting "the demand is real" and "the capital is not Nvidia revenue," but Wall Street is visibly unconvinced, and this is now the central fault line in whether the AI infrastructure boom is self-sustaining or self-referential.

Researchers Show a Side-Channel That Extracts OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google's Hidden Reasoning

A MATS/ELLIS/Max Planck team found that providers' encrypted chain-of-thought blocks are interchangeable across sessions and models — feed one into a weaker, less-safeguarded model from the same provider and it will decode and output the reasoning in plaintext.
