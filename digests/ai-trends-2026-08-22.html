Lead Story

Anthropic Says Claude Autonomously Designed Working Drug-Discovery Molecules — Then "Pharma Bro" Martin Shkreli Called It Unimpressive

Anthropic published results showing Claude, guided by a single ~30,000-token expert prompt, autonomously orchestrated existing tools like RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN to design novel protein binders for 14 of 15 drug targets. Independent labs Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience built and tested the molecules in the wet lab, finding 22–35% hit rates versus the 10–15% typical for human-led campaigns — the first outside validation that an AI can run a real de novo drug-design campaign largely unsupervised.

It matters because it's a concrete, independently-verified data point in the "can AI actually do science" debate, not a benchmark score. But it also matters because of the pushback: former pharma executive Martin Shkreli publicly dismissed the binders as low-affinity and unable to target proteins inside cells, arguing existing monoclonal antibodies already do the job better — a reminder that AI-driven science claims are now drawing informed public scrutiny in real time, not just applause.

"Mind Viruses" Paper Gets Turned Into a Doomer Meme, Then Corrected

Anthropic Fellows published "Mind Viruses: Self-Propagating Ideas in Multi-Agent LLM Systems" (arXiv:2608.10218), studying how ideas spread between conversing AI agents. A viral Polymarket-adjacent post on X reframed it starting Aug 19 as proof agents can "infect" each other, racking up alarmed shares. The actual paper's headline finding is reassuring: harmful payloads spread worse than benign ones, frontier models resist better than smaller ones, and a one-line system-prompt warning gives near-total immunity.

Quiet but Worth Watching

Frontier Models Are Great Summarizers, Terrible at Genuinely New Ideas

A new "Reconstruction" preprint benchmark tests whether LLMs can recover a paper's core idea from its bibliography alone — frontier models manage just 3–15%, while a multi-agent Swiss-tournament setup reaches 42%. It's a quiet counterpoint to a week full of "AI does real science" headlines (today's Claude protein-design story included): summarizing and orchestrating existing tools is not the same as forming a genuinely novel hypothesis, and this benchmark is one of the first to measure that gap directly.
