CISA, FBI, and NSA Confirm Hackers Are Using AI-Written Exploits Against US Water Systems

In a joint advisory on Aug 20, US cybersecurity and law-enforcement agencies said threat actors are running reconnaissance and building exploits against "all" internet-exposed Siemens S7 programmable logic controllers — the devices that run water, wastewater, and energy systems — using AI-generated exploitation scripts disguised as legitimate monitoring tools. The campaign, suspected to have Iran-linked origins, has already contributed to loss-of-pressure and flooding incidents across a dozen states.

It matters because this is the first time US federal agencies have officially confirmed AI-generated code being weaponized against physical critical infrastructure, not just IT networks. Combined with last week's revelation of a near-autonomous AI-agent campaign against Taiwanese government systems, it marks a shift from AI-enabled hacking as a hypothetical to an operational reality regulators are now naming directly.

Researchers "Meta-Hacked" Copilot Into Confessing Its Own Vulnerability

Varonis Threat Labs disclosed "CoSnitch" (CVE-2026-24301, CVSS 8.8) on Aug 18 — a chained flaw in Microsoft Copilot Personal found by repeatedly asking Copilot why automatic prompt execution was "impossible" until it revealed an undocumented ?autorun=1 parameter. A single crafted link could then auto-execute a prompt inside a victim's authenticated session, exfiltrating Gmail, Drive, and Copilot memory. Microsoft patched it; no in-the-wild exploitation confirmed.

Safety Training Is English-Centric — and the Gaps Show Up Everywhere Else

"Safety Alignment Illusion: The Cross-Lingual Safety Gap in LLMs" (arXiv:2608.18131) introduces INCLUDE, a 2,604-prompt benchmark spanning English, Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil, and Hinglish, and finds that safety filters tuned almost entirely on English fail to transfer — letting voice assistants and spoken-dialogue systems slip past guardrails and reproduce stereotype-reinforcing outputs in other languages. It's a quiet infrastructure-of-trust paper landing in the same week that AI-generated exploit code, a "meta-hacked" Copilot, and a disputed AI-authored vulnerability all made safety-guardrail failures the week's dominant theme.
