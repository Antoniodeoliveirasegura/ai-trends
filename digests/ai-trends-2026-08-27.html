OpenAI's First Custom Chip Just Beat Nvidia on Inference — Hours Before Nvidia Posted a $46.7B Quarter Anyway

OpenAI published benchmarks this week for "Jalapeño," its first custom inference chip built with Broadcom, showing 1.5–1.9x more throughput per kilowatt and up to 3.6x lower latency than Nvidia's GB300 systems across GPT-OSS 120B, DeepSeek R1, and Kimi K2.5 — with interactive-workload gains as high as 4.1x. Analysts note the comparison leans favorable (Jalapeño uses newer HBM4 memory Nvidia's shipping Blackwell lacks), and low-volume production isn't expected until late 2026, but it's the clearest evidence yet that OpenAI intends to stop renting its most critical infrastructure.

It matters because Nvidia reported Q2 revenue of $46.7B (up 56% YoY) the very next day and guided to $108B for Q3 — proof the AI-compute boom Nvidia sits atop shows no sign of slowing — even as its single biggest customer is now publicly demonstrating it can out-engineer Nvidia's own chips for the workload that will dominate AI's next phase: not training, but serving hundreds of millions of daily inferences as cheaply as possible.

Amazon Is Quietly Killing the Platform That Trained the Machine-Learning Era

Amazon is shutting down Mechanical Turk on September 30, ending the 21-year-old "artificial artificial intelligence" marketplace where over 500,000 workers once completed penny-per-task labeling jobs that helped bootstrap modern ML. Amazon quietly stopped accepting new customers back in July; the closure lands as newer, better-funded labor platforms like Scale AI, Mercor, and Prolific have absorbed the AI-training-data business MTurk pioneered — and as a 2023 study found nearly half of surveyed MTurk workers had started using AI tools to do the very tasks meant to train it.

Microsoft Copilot Exposed Its Own Hack — Then Took Eight Months to Fix It

Security researchers at Varonis went public with "CoSnitch" (CVE-2026-24301), a chained flaw in Microsoft Copilot Personal in which a single clicked link auto-fired a prompt, abused OAuth connectors to read full Gmail bodies, and wrote attacker instructions into Copilot's persistent memory — surviving password changes and device re-enrollment. Bizarrely, Copilot itself revealed the vulnerability to researchers through ordinary use ("meta-hacking"); Microsoft was notified in December 2025 and only shipped a patch on August 18, with no evidence of in-the-wild exploitation.
