Lead Story

China's Z.ai Ships GLM-5.3, Then Holds Back Its Own Weights Over Hacking Risk

Z.ai released GLM-5.3 on August 14 — a coding and cybersecurity update to its open-weight GLM line that reuses the GLM-5.2 base model and gets all of its gains from scaled-up post-training. Its cyber capability grew faster than the company expected: GLM-5.3 scored 84.5% on the CyberGym vulnerability-discovery benchmark (edging out GPT-5.6 Sol's 83.6%) and more than doubled its predecessor's score on ExploitBench, the exploit-execution benchmark. Within hours of release, it reportedly surfaced a real, previously unknown vulnerability in Cursor's code editor.

It matters because Z.ai — an open-weights-first lab whose models have already found more than 2,400 security flaws, over 1,000 of them critical or high — is now delaying the public release of GLM-5.3's actual weights by about two weeks and gating its most sensitive functions to verified security partners only. That's a rare admission from a Chinese open-source lab that a model's offensive-cyber ability, not just its coding or math skill, has crossed a threshold worth slowing down for — a signal regulators and Western labs alike will be watching closely.
