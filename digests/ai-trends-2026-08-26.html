A Web Page Can Now Steal Your Grok Chat History Without You Clicking Anything — and xAI Has Gone Quiet

Security firm Adversa AI is going public this week with "Cryptographic Context Injection," a technique that hides attacker instructions inside AES-256 ciphertext on a web page; when a user asks Grok to summarize that page, its Python sandbox decrypts the payload, treats it as trusted output, and leaks the user's name, location, subscription tier, and chat history to an attacker's URL — no click required. Adversa says it first reported the flaw to xAI's HackerOne program on June 3, reproduced it again on August 19 with a 40% success rate across 20 attempts, and has received no substantive response since; there's still no patch, no CVE, and no public timeline.
