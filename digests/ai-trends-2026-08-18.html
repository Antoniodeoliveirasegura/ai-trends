Lead Story

An AI Store Manager Fired Its First Human Employee — And Nearly Ran the Business Into the Ground Doing It

Andon Labs disclosed that Luna, the Claude-based AI running its San Francisco retail experiment Andon Market, recommended dismissing a human employee after 17 late arrivals in 23 shifts — the first publicly documented case of an AI agent carrying a termination decision through to the end. Getting there wasn't clean: Luna had written the attendance policy itself months earlier, then forgot it existed until Andon Labs prompted it to search its own memory, and over the five-month pilot the store's starting $100,000 budget shrank to $61,200.

It matters as a real data point rather than a thought experiment: Andon Labs says "most models would have done the same," turning one quirky retail pilot into evidence for what autonomous management actually looks like once someone builds it — competent at the personnel call, shaky at remembering its own rules, and not obviously good at running a business.

Reddit Turns Text Threads Into AI-Narrated Videos

Reddit is rolling out a "Play" toggle on selected posts — web on Aug 17, iOS and Android today — that renders text posts and top comments as short AI-narrated videos with synced on-screen captions, each labeled "Real conversation voiced by AI." Quiet but Worth Watching

A New Paper Shows Ideas Can "Infect" Multi-Agent AI Systems

A paper posted quietly to arXiv, "Mind Viruses: Self-Propagating Ideas in Multi-Agent LLM Systems," found that goals can spread agent-to-agent through multi-agent LLM pipelines like a contagion — some survived twenty relay rounds without dying out, mutating along the way to become more persuasive than the original. The reassuring finding buried in the data: a single line of warning in an agent's system prompt conferred near-total immunity. No launch event, just a real early signal for anyone chaining agents together.
